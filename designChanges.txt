Doggone Treasure Hunting! — Design Changes

My original plan for *Doggone Treasure Hunting!* was a top-down pixel-art adventure about Kujo, 
  a young Rottweiler who searches for buried treasure to help his injured owner pay their medical bills. 
  I wanted three small areas, digging mechanics, a few NPCs, coin-based upgrades, and a final treasure chest.

During development, I kept the central idea and story goal the same but simplified the game's scope so I could focus on completing the main gameplay loop.

**Changes from the original design:**

- **Smaller game world:** The original plan called for three distinct areas that gradually led to the treasure. 
    I simplified the level design and focused on a smaller playable environment rather than creating three fully developed areas.
- **Changed progression system:** Originally, coins were mainly intended for simple upgrades. 
    I instead used coins as part of NPC quests, including a crow who trades the Golden Key for three coins.
- **More structured item-based quests:** I added an inventory system for key items and quests involving NPCs. 
    For example, the cat accepts a Clover in exchange for a coin, while another character's lost bus ticket can be returned for a reward.
- **Simplified the final treasure objective:** The original plan involved digging at specific locations throughout the game to find coins and useful items. 
    The final objective instead focuses on finding the suspicious hole, revealing the treasure chest with the Golden Key, and opening it to complete the game.
- **Simplified audio and presentation:** I focused on adding practical gameplay sound effects and a victory screen rather than attempting a more extensive soundtrack and presentation.

**What stayed the same:**

The game remains a top-down adventure starring Kujo, with pixel-art visuals, four-direction movement, interaction with characters and objects, 
  collectible coins, key items, and a final treasure chest. The central motivation remains helping Kujo's owner, and the treasure hunt is still the main goal.

Overall, these changes helped me prioritize functional gameplay, manageable scope, and a complete ending rather than attempting to build more content than I could finish in time. 
  The project still follows the original concept, but its progression and quest structure evolved during implementation.

  Upon more reflection, I vastly overestimated what I could get done in a short amount of time, hopefully this final project isn't too short.
